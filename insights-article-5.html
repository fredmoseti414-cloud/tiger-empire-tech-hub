Freelancing From Kisii: What Actually Works

Freelancing doesn't require living in Nairobi or having expensive equipment. What it does require is a realistic starting point — and most people skip that step, jumping straight to "which platform should I join" before they've figured out what they're actually offering.

Start with one skill you can already do

Writing, data entry, graphic design, transcription, basic bookkeeping — pick something you can already deliver today, not something you plan to learn eventually. Your first few clients build the track record that makes the next ones easier to get.

A connection matters more than a perfect profile

Freelance platforms are crowded, and a new profile with no reviews is easy to overlook. Local networks — people who already know your work, or a small business that needs something done — are often a faster route to a first paying client than a global platform.

Price for the work, not the guilt

A common mistake is under-pricing out of fear of losing the client. Decide your rate based on the time and skill the work takes, not on what feels comfortable to ask for. It's easier to start firm than to raise prices later with the same client.

Getting set up properly

A clean CV, a simple portfolio, and a professional online presence all help more than people expect. Our Freelancing & Online Income course and Personal & Career Solutions cover exactly this groundwork.
