AI Tools Worth Learning First, If You're Starting From Zero

Most people's first contact with AI is ChatGPT, and that's a reasonable place to start — but it's not the only tool worth knowing, and it isn't always the best fit for every task. If you're building AI skills from nothing, the order you learn things in matters more than how many tools you try.

Start with one tool, not five

It's tempting to try every AI app you hear about. In practice, you'll learn faster by picking one general-purpose assistant — ChatGPT or Claude both work well — and using it for a few real tasks you already do: drafting a message, summarising a document, brainstorming ideas. Understanding how to write a clear instruction (a "prompt") transfers to almost every other AI tool you'll use later.

Then add tools for specific jobs

  • Canva for design work — posters, social media graphics, simple branding.
  • A writing assistant (built into ChatGPT or Claude) for CVs, cover letters and business copy.
  • Spreadsheet AI features if your work involves data — most modern spreadsheet tools now have some AI assistance built in.

What AI won't do for you

AI tools are fastest at producing a first draft, not a finished, accurate result. Anything with real consequences — a legal document, a government application, financial figures — still needs a human to check it carefully before it goes anywhere.
